Beyond the phosphor screens, there was a flat world

that harboured glee and obvious endings.

Cat-and-rat conflicts had no casualties. 

A roadrunner bent gravity, and mythical monsters sprang from spheres.

Tin Spinach built superhuman sailors and silly siblings quarrelled in a science lab.

An octopus and his mates made strange bedfellows, and a cowardly dog kept saving the day.

A bird, a bunny, a dog and a duck,  were spoiled rotten by a plump granny.

Little girls and men in capes fought for their tainted towns.

Gadgets grew legs and walked away, on secret missions to save their keepers.

Gobbling rice cakes firmed by time,I begged Mom for an extra hour, to let me stay tranced by this pendulum of pretence.

I prayed to the gods, old and new, to stunt all growth and trap me in these plastic realms.

But then a freight train named Thirty hit.

Heart and brain divorced, and co-parented my frigid inner child.

Aftershocks of life’s lobotomy left no fuel for the fire in my belly.

Nostalgia honed its double-edged sword and swore to stab if I ever looked back. 

I yearned for a counterfeit world, where colours stayed static, season after season.

Each day had closure and shapes were smooth.

Wish I could get hurt over and over, while mishaps were still an onomatopoeia.

Higher dimensions are none of my business. 

Let me dwell in a weak plot and dance under poor pixels.

Single-celled, unevolved, run by rule-based algorithms.

Let me be a dot, much worse, a line.
